# Findings Presentation — Video Walkthrough

A 2–3 minute video presenting the finance findings to the team, paired with
the [findings deck](./Urban_Hamster_Case3_Finance_Findings.pptx). Slides show
both the presenter and the deck; no code is shown — the audience is the
non-technical finance team.

## Speaker Notes

**Cover.** Kent asked three things: how much we've actually grown, whether our
pricing is holding up, and how we compare to competitors. Five years of data
tell a good story — we're growing fast, pricing is disciplined, and the biggest
opportunity now is pricing to the market.

**Slide 1 — Growth.** Revenue went from under $200K in 2019 to over $4M in 2023
— roughly 21×, and it sped up last year (66% → 91% growth). But average order
value never moved; it sat near $86 every year. So ~96% of growth came from more
orders, not higher prices — price is an unused lever.

**Slide 2 — Margins.** Not one product sells below cost, and 99.99% of the
catalog follows the cost-plus-50% rule. Only four miss it, all at ~49% — and
all four are two-piece sets, a bundling quirk we can re-price quickly. Two
flags: the rule prices off our costs (not the market), and it checks list price
(not what we collect after discounts). Recommend tracking realized margin.

**Slide 3 — Competitors.** Don't track all 29,000 products — start with the few
hundred that drive most revenue, plus our biggest and highest-ticket categories.
Check monthly, weekly for seasonal items. Pull competitor pricing from the
Snowflake Marketplace and join it to our own data. Bottom line: the next dollar
of margin comes from pricing to the market, not just to cost.
